# Copywriting for landing pages

## Principles

- Write for one specific reader. Name their situation.
- Lead with the outcome, then the mechanism.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Cut adjectives that any competitor could also use.
- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Be honest about what exists today and what is planned.

## Headline patterns

| Pattern | Example shape |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome for audience | "Ship a clean changelog without leaving your editor" |
| Before and after | "From scattered notes to one searchable record" |
| Direct description | "A command-line formatter for SQL" |
| Constraint as feature | "Works offline. Stores nothing on a server." |

Avoid: stacked buzzwords ("seamless, intelligent, next-generation"), vague superlatives ("the best"), and clever lines that hide what the product is.

## Subheads

One or two sentences. Add the missing piece: who it is for, how it works, or what makes it different.

## Calls to action

- Primary: verb plus object. "Start a project", "Install the CLI", "Book a table".
- Secondary: lower emphasis. "Read the docs", "See how it works".
- Repeat the primary action after the key proof, and at the end.
- Say what happens next when it is not obvious ("No account needed", "Takes about two minutes").

## Proof

Acceptable proof: screenshots and code from the real product, specific facts, documented limits, user-supplied numbers, linked case studies, public repositories.

Not acceptable: invented testimonials, made-up customer names or logos, unsupported statistics, fake ratings, fabricated press mentions. When proof does not exist, shrink the claim or show how it works instead.

## Microcopy

- Form labels say what to enter. Errors say what went wrong and how to fix it.
- Empty states explain what will appear and how to start.
- Button labels do not change meaning between states without telling the user.

## Voice

Pick a register (plain, dry, warm, formal) in the concept phase and keep it. Read the final copy aloud once; rewrite anything you would not say to the reader's face.

## Placeholder policy

If you must invent copy to complete the layout, make it plausible but unmistakably generic, mark it with a code comment such as `TODO(content): replace`, and list it in your summary.
