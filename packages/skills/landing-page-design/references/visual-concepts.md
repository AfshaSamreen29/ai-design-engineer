# Visual concepts

A concept is a short, specific description of how the page should look and feel, chosen because of the product and audience. Its job is to make dozens of small decisions consistent.

## How to choose

1. List three words a visitor should feel. Reject generic words (modern, clean, professional, sleek). Prefer words with texture: dry, warm, brisk, archival, playful, severe, tactile.
2. Find a reference world: a real physical or printed domain related to the product or its audience. Borrow its structure (grid, rules, type contrast, color restraint), not its clip art.
3. Pick one signature device that will repeat and be recognizable.
4. Write what the page is *not*. Naming the generic default makes it easier to avoid.

## Directions to start from

These are starting points, not a menu to pick from blindly. Combine, adapt, or discard them.

| Direction | Feels | Typical moves | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial | Considered, literate | Large serif headings, narrow text columns, pull quotes, thin rules | Tools for writers, research, premium services |
| Technical manual | Precise, trustworthy | Mono accents, numbered sections, diagrams, tables | Developer tools, infrastructure, security |
| Ledger or ticket | Orderly, evidentiary | Ruled rows, tabular numerals, stamps, strong grid | Finance, compliance, logistics |
| Field notes | Human, warm | Handwritten accents used sparingly, paper tones, annotated images | Local businesses, education, craft |
| Specimen sheet | Confident, typographic | Oversized type, character grids, minimal color | Type, design, creative studios |
| Bold poster | Loud, energetic | Flat color fields, huge headings, tight crops | Events, consumer launches, youth brands |
| Quiet product | Calm, focused | Lots of space, one screenshot, restrained palette | Single-purpose apps, wellness, focus tools |

## Keeping variety

- Change at least three of these from your last project: layout grid, heading family, primary neutral, accent hue, hero structure, section rhythm, illustration style.
- Vary hero structure: split with product on one side, full-bleed type, a working demo, an annotated diagram, a plain text opening followed by evidence.
- Vary section rhythm: alternate wide and narrow, dense and airy. Not every section needs a heading, a paragraph, and a card grid.

## Color notes

- Start with a neutral pair (background and ink) that already has character: warm paper and near-black, cool gray and navy, cream and forest.
- Choose one accent and test it on the background for contrast before committing.
- Use the accent for actions, key data, and focus states, not as decoration.

## Typography notes

- Pair contrast with a reason: a serif heading with a sans body for editorial; a grotesque heading with mono accents for technical.
- Limit to two families plus mono. Use weight and size for hierarchy before adding another family.
- Always specify a fallback stack that matches the metrics reasonably well.
